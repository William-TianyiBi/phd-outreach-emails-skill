# Lab Funding Search

Use this when reverse-researching a lab before drafting PhD outreach. The goal is not to prove whether a professor can fund Tianyi; it is to create a dated, evidence-based funding visibility snapshot.

## Search Order

1. Start with official pages: lab homepage, PI profile, join/openings page, people page, news, CV, annual reports, and publication acknowledgments.
2. Search official funding databases by PI name, institution, lab keywords, and robotics topic keywords.
3. Check institution news, center pages, project pages, award announcements, and sponsor press releases for major awards not indexed in federal databases.
4. Compare visible funding with lab size, current PhD students, recent PhD intake, and recent publication activity.
5. Record sources searched even when no award is found. Missing public data means `Unknown`, not no funding.

## Funding Databases

For United States targets, search:

- NSF Award Search: PI/co-PI name, institution, robotics/manipulation/autonomy keywords.
- NIH RePORTER: PI/person and organization, especially for medical robotics, rehabilitation, haptics, biomechanics, sensing, and human-subject work.
- DOE, NASA, USDA NIFA, DARPA, ONR, ARO, AFOSR, and DoD/Minerva/URI pages when the lab topic fits autonomy, field robotics, controls, agriculture, defense, or cyber-physical systems.
- University research news and center pages for industry or foundation funding.

For non-US targets, use the closest official databases and sponsor pages: Hong Kong RGC, NSFC, EU CORDIS, UKRI Gateway, KAUST/MBZUAI/university news, national research council portals, foundation pages, and lab CVs.

## What To Capture

For each visible grant or award:

- Sponsor, award title, PI/co-PI role, amount if public, start/end dates, project URL, and access date.
- Whether the topic plausibly supports the lab direction Tianyi is contacting about.
- Whether the grant is active, recent but expired, stale, or only indirectly related.

For each professor, produce one compact snapshot:

```text
Funding: Confirmed active NSF award on robot learning through 2027; PI role confirmed; funding risk Low.
Funding search: NSF Award Search, lab news, PI CV searched on YYYY-MM-DD.
```

If nothing current is found:

```text
Funding: No active public grants found in searched official sources; funding risk Unknown.
Funding search: NSF, NIH RePORTER, lab news, PI profile searched on YYYY-MM-DD.
```

## Risk Labels

Use `Low`, `Medium`, `High`, or `Unknown` from `source-quality.md`.

- `Low`: active/recent grants and plausible student support path are visible.
- `Medium`: some signals exist, but awards are stale, indirect, or support path is unclear.
- `High`: little current evidence, expired awards, unclear appointment status, or lab size appears unsupported by visible funding.
- `Unknown`: searched sources are insufficient; do not turn missing public records into a negative claim.

## Student-First Use

Funding research should shape the outreach route:

- If the lab has active funding and current PhD students, prefer emailing a topic-relevant current PhD student and Cc the professor.
- If current students are hard to identify, use publication coauthors, lab alumni, project pages, and funding personnel as weak leads, then verify against official/lab-controlled pages.
- If Tianyi asks for Chinese students, record only public China-background or Chinese-language signals. Do not infer nationality, ethnicity, citizenship, or language ability from names, photos, or appearance.

## Common Mistakes

- Writing "no funding" because no database hit appeared quickly. Write `No active public grants found in searched sources`.
- Treating a co-PI award as full PI funding without noting the role.
- Ignoring grant end dates.
- Counting unrelated grants as strong fit.
- Using old lab rosters as current membership without dates.
